## The ADHD Founder's Dilemma

ADHD brains are wired differently. We're _<u>not</u> broken_. We're _<u>not</u> lazy_. We're _<u>not</u> undisciplined_.

But we <u>do</u> struggle with:
>
- **Executive function** - Knowing what to do but not being able to start
- **Rejection sensitivity** - Feeling the weight of judgment more intensely
- **Time blindness** - "I'll do it later" becomes "I never did it"
- **Dopamine dependency** - Tasks without immediate reward feel impossible

Traditional accountability systems assume you're neurotypical. They don't work for ADHD brains because they don't account for how we actually experience pressure, commitment, and follow-through.

**Here's what <u>does</u> work:**
>
- Small, structured commitments with immediate feedback
- A safe group where failure is expected and normalized
- Anonymous accountability that gradually becomes known accountability
- A system that lets you win repeatedly until your brain rewires

_**That's PEACH-Flow.**_

## Built on Ancient Philosophy and Modern Science
Jordan, Lombardi, and Epictetus aren't just inspirational quotes. They're the foundation of PEACH-Flow's methodology.

**Soft outside, hard at the core.**

A peach is not just sweet. At its centre is something that doesn't yield. PEACH-Flow is built on the same duality: warm enough to be honest in, demanding enough to actually change something. Ubuntu says *I am because we are*. Stoicism says discomfort is not the enemy, it's fuel for growth. Like fire consuming wood, the obstacle doesn't block the way, it is the way. Together they're the foundation PEACH-Flow is built on.

**Separate the feeling from the action.**

Procrastination is not a character flaw. It is emotional dysregulation. Research by Pychyl & Flett (2012) links chronic procrastination directly to difficulty regulating negative emotion, not poor time management. The practice is not to eliminate the discomfort. It is to act while it is still present. First time, showing up is enough. Get your reps in and you will get better.

**Pressure reveals character. Repeated pressure builds it.**

The Stoics practiced voluntary hardship, deliberate exposure to discomfort to build tolerance. Sports science calls this _pressure inoculation_: graduated exposure to stress in a controlled environment increases performance under real conditions (Driskell & Johnston, 1998). The Growth Sprint is that environment. You are not performing. You are training.

**Failure is shared data, not individual verdict.**

When one member stalls, the group helps diagnose the bottleneck without shame. This mirrors what Annie Duke calls a truthseeking charter in _Thinking in Bets_: a small group committed to accuracy over comfort. Amy Edmondson's psychological safety research (Harvard, 1999) shows high-performing teams are not conflict-free. They are environments where people feel safe enough to take interpersonal risks. I grow because the community grows.
